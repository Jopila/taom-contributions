# Dale Lord Expansion

## Overview

Dale (`Kingdom.sturgia`) grows from 9 clans and 48 adult lords to 14 clans and 75 adult lords, which gives it 30 party slots instead of about 18. The change is data only: five new clans, 27 new lords, two tier raises and two re-seated castles. It needs a new campaign, because Heroes and Factions load at campaign start.

## Why This Exists

- **Vanilla behavior:** a kingdom fields as many lord parties as its clans' tiers allow. `DefaultClanTierModel` gives tier 1 and 2 clans one party, tier 3 and 4 two, and tier 5 and 6 three. Lords added inside an existing clan add no parties.
- **TAOM requirement:** Dale had 9 clans, 48 adult lords and about 18 lord parties, against 130 adult lords for Dol Guldur and 120 for Rhun. The major-kingdom median is 75. Dale needed to field armies at a comparable scale.
- **Without this feature:** Dale stays the thinnest major kingdom on the map and loses wars of attrition to kingdoms with several times its parties.

## Architecture

### Design Challenge

Lord count is not party count. Because party slots follow clan tier, the fix has to add clans or raise tiers; extra lords in the existing clans would only add names.

### Solution Approach

Pure data, generated by `tools/author_dale_lords.py` (dry-run by default, `--apply` writes, idempotent). It adds five clans `clan_sturgia_10` to `clan_sturgia_14` and 27 lords `lord_S10_*` to `lord_S14_*` to `characters/lords.xml` and `characters/heroes.xml`, and `spclans.xslt` raises `clan_sturgia_8` and `clan_sturgia_9` to tier 3. The new ids are pinned in `CULTURES['dale']['canonical']` in `tools/apply_culture_skills_traits.py`, so the skill pass keeps them.

## Dependencies

- `DefaultClanTierModel` (engine): maps clan tier to party count.
- The live TAOM_Map module: the settlements the new clans own.

## Tests

No new C# tests; the change is data. `python tools/validate_moduledata.py` checks the new references.

## How to Add More Dale Lords

1. Edit the spec in `tools/author_dale_lords.py`.
2. Run it without flags to read the dry-run, then with `--apply`.
3. Pin any new ids in `CULTURES['dale']['canonical']` in `tools/apply_culture_skills_traits.py`.
4. If the goal is more armies, raise a clan tier or add a clan; more lords in a clan add none.
